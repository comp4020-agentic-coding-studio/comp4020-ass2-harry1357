Week 3 asked you to fit a damage formula to ten hits and predict the eleventh.
This is the written version of that, on a game nobody has handed you the numbers
for.

## What you hand in

Three things. The log: ten hits, with every held-constant input recorded
alongside. The fit: a formula, plus the curve shape you assumed before fitting
and why. The prediction: the eleventh hit's value, a tolerance you committed to
in advance, and the number you actually got.

One page of method holds it together — what you varied, what you controlled, and
what you'd do differently.

## How it's marked

Mostly on the method, which is the part that transfers. A teardown that guessed
a cubic, fitted it, predicted within 5% and said so in advance is a strong
submission. So is one that guessed wrong, missed by 40%, and correctly diagnoses
why from its own log.

A submission with a good fit and no stated tolerance scores poorly, because it
can't be wrong, and something that can't be wrong isn't a model. This is the
same standard week 4 applies to expected values and the final design document
applies to everything.
