# Speed Is a Reset, Not a Rate

Week 2 — the initiative counter

---

## Last week

A round is a container.

Commands go in, an order comes out, everything resolves, repeat.

Pokémon sorts. Dragon Quest rolls.

---

## This week

Final Fantasy X has no rounds.

---

## Two ways to be fast

**ATB** — a bar fills continuously. Full bar, you act.

Speed is throughput. A fast character fills faster.

---

## Two ways to be fast

**CTB** — a counter ticks down. Zero, you act, counter resets.

All counters tick at the same rate.

Speed is the size of the reset.

---

{/* _class: banner */}

## Agility doesn't make you faster

It makes the gap smaller.

---

## The formula

```
counter = tickSpeed × rank × hasteMultiplier
```

Three inputs. Everything in the fight comes out of them.

---

## Input 1: tick speed

Agility goes into a lookup table, tick speed comes out.

A table is a step function — gains arrive in jumps, and the designer
chooses where the steps are.

Find where yours flattens before you spend on it.

---

## Input 2: rank

| Action | Rank |
| ------ | ---- |
| Change equipment, escape | 1 |
| Item, Defend | 2 |
| Attack, most abilities | 3 |
| … up to | 8 |

---

## What rank buys

A rank 1 action costs a third of a rank 3.

Three cheap turns in the time somebody else takes one.

This is why Defend is a real move in FFX.

---

## Rounding

Every step rounds down.

At high Agility with Haste, two rank 1 actions can cost fewer ticks
than one rank 2.

That's the arithmetic, not a bug.

---

## Input 3: Haste

Halves the reset. Slow doubles it.

Casting it also halves the **current** counter, once.

Dispelling it doesn't give the counter back.

---

## So Haste is worth most early

Its value is measured in resets avoided, not speed gained.

---

## The prediction

Tick speeds plus ranks gives you the next five turns.

FFX prints the same list on the HUD.

Rare thing: you can check your model against the game's.

---

## Where the list breaks

It assumes everyone takes a rank 3 action.

Defend, an item, a Haste, a Slow — invalid from there on.

Ties don't break it: fixed order, party first, Cid last.

---

{/* _class: banner */}

## A system that asks you to plan five turns ahead

can't then flip a coin.

---

## This week's activity

One row per combatant, one column per tick.

Implement the formula. Feed it a real fight. Compare.

Then cast Haste at tick 0 and at tick 40.

---

## The failure worth meeting

Implement Haste as a change to the **rate**.

Your model drifts slowly and stays plausible for about a dozen ticks.

Better to meet that in a spreadsheet than in a fight.
