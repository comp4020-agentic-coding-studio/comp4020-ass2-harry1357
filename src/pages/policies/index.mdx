# Policies and support

One page, so the four briefs don't each carry their own version of the rules and
slowly disagree with one another. These are the course's rules; anything
university-wide is on the university's own pages, not restated here.

## Late work

Five percent of the item off per working day, up to five days, after which it
isn't marked.

The fight logs are the exception, because they're a running artefact: a missing
week costs that week's sheet and nothing else. Twelve sheets handed in on time
with one gap beats eleven handed in late.

## Collaboration

Your log is yours. The twelve tables are your reading of twelve fights, and two
students who logged the same fight should be able to disagree about a row — that
disagreement is most of what week 1 is for.

Your models can be compared. Take somebody's spreadsheet apart, argue about
whether their Haste halves the reset or the rate, rebuild yours after losing the
argument. A model is a claim about a system, and claims get better in public.
Say whose model you looked at; that costs you nothing.

The design document is yours. Week 12 has you implement a classmate's and hand
back a question log — their document stays theirs, and what you submit is your
implementation and your questions.

## Using an AI assistant

Allowed for anything you can verify against a recorded fight. Not allowed as a
substitute for the log.

That's the whole rule, and it follows from the course's position rather than
from anywhere else. If an assistant writes a spreadsheet formula, you can step
the model forward and check its turn order against the recording — the fight is
the ground truth, and a wrong formula fails visibly. If an assistant writes your
ten logged hits, there's nothing to check them against, and the item you're
submitting no longer exists.

So: fitting a curve with an assistant, fine. Generating the numbers you fitted
it to, not fine. Drafting the prose of a design document with one, fine.
Inventing the damage formula it specifies, pointless — that formula is the
assignment.

Every submission carries a short note naming what you used and for what.
"Wrote the sheet formulas with an assistant, checked the turn order against the
week 6 recording myself" is complete. A submission with no note claims you used
nothing.

## Extensions

Ask before the deadline if you can, after it if you couldn't. A sentence saying
what happened and what date you want is enough; no documentation is required for
anything under a week.

The design document is the one piece with a hard edge, because marking has to
finish — an extension past the last teaching day needs a reason the university
recognises, and that process is theirs, not this page's.

## What "in numbers" means

It appears in every brief, so: a submission is in numbers when somebody else can
recompute your result without asking you anything.

A formula with every term defined is in numbers. "Damage scales with Strength"
is not. A crit chance of 1/24 at 1.5× is in numbers. "Crits are rare but
satisfying" is not. A threshold of 300% with a decay rate is in numbers. "The
gauge drains fairly quickly" is not.

Prose is welcome everywhere and does a job no table does. It just can't stand in
for the arithmetic, because the arithmetic is the thing being marked.

## Getting help

Idris first for anything practical — a spreadsheet misbehaving, a fit that
won't converge, a recording you can't find. Marisol for the design document,
marking and dates.

If a question has gone unanswered for two working days, send it again. That
isn't rudeness, it's the agreed escalation.
